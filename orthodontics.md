# Orthodontics
**Weight: 6% · Official domains:** screening, space management.

## 1. Malocclusion essentials
- **Angle Class I:** normal molar relationship with potential crowding/rotations; **Class II:** mandibular molar distal to Class I; **Class III:** mesial to Class I.
- **Class II Division 1** often increased overjet/proclined upper incisors; **Division 2** often retroclined maxillary central incisors and deep bite.
- **Overjet** is horizontal incisor overlap; **overbite** is vertical overlap. Negative overjet may be present in anterior crossbite.
- Distinguish **skeletal, dental and functional** sources of discrepancy by facial profile, occlusion, functional shift, growth and cephalometric assessment when appropriate.
- **Posterior crossbite with functional shift** warrants timely screening; consider mandibular midline displacement and occlusal interference.
- Early interceptive issues: traumatic deep bite, impacted/ectopic eruption, severe overjet with trauma risk, crossbites and loss of arch length.

## 2. Mixed dentition and eruption
- Review **normal eruption sequence** and differences between dental and chronological age; use radiographs when clinically indicated.
- Primary molars help maintain arch length; premature loss can permit mesial drift and crowding.
- Before a space maintainer, assess **which tooth was lost, successor development, permanent molar eruption, remaining space, opposing occlusion, hygiene and cooperation**.
- **Distal shoe** is a specialized appliance option for early loss of a primary second molar **before first permanent molar eruption**; it requires careful indication and follow-up.
- **Leeway space** = difference between the combined mesiodistal widths of primary canine/molars and successors; useful for crowding/space management.
- **Ectopic eruption** and blocked-out canines require evaluation of neighboring roots, position and timing.

## 3. Screening and referral choices
- Document **overjet, overbite, crossbite, crowding, spacing, midlines, molar/canine relationships**, and periodontal/hygiene status.
- Assess growth status and skeletal relationship before selecting an intervention.
- During active caries/poor hygiene, address disease control before elective orthodontic bonding where indicated.
- **Retention** is important after active orthodontics; relapse risks include growth, periodontal support and habits.
- Avoid treatment based solely on a one-word Angle classification; integrate functional and skeletal findings.

## 4. Pediatric orthodontic appliances: high-yield identification

**First decide whether space maintenance is indicated.** Check the missing primary tooth, chronology of loss, dental age, successor presence/root development, eruption timing, remaining space, arch length discrepancy, occlusion, radiographs when needed, abutment restorability, plaque control, cooperation, and medical risks. **Space maintenance preserves space already present; space regaining actively recovers space already lost.** An appliance does not automatically follow every early primary tooth extraction.

| Appliance | Recognize it / common indication | "Spot the error" in a case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Band-and-loop** | Fixed **unilateral** maintainer: band on an intact abutment with passive loop contacting the adjacent tooth; a common option after loss of one primary molar when the first permanent molar is already erupted as needed. | Loop is **subgingival**, cuts gingiva, blocks successor eruption, fails to contact the supporting tooth, or the band is loose/decemented. If space has already collapsed, a passive loop will not regain it. |
| **Crown-and-loop** | As above, but the abutment is a **stainless-steel crown** because the supporting primary molar needs full coverage. | A band is placed on a severely carious/nonrestorable abutment instead of first managing tooth prognosis; open/cement-defective crown margin or gingival impingement. |
| **Distal shoe** | **Primary second molar lost before permanent first molar erupts**: subgingival guide helps direct eruption of the first permanent molar. | Routine band-and-loop used with **no erupted distal abutment**; guide placed without assessing developing first-molar position; excessively deep/unmonitored subgingival extension or use without considering bleeding/immunologic conditions. Requires careful radiographic/clinical follow-up. |
| **Lower lingual holding arch (LLHA)** | Bilateral mandibular space management, usually anchored to erupted **permanent first molars** with a passive wire adapted near lower incisors; may preserve leeway space. | Wire causes lingual mucosal trauma, moves incisors unintentionally, interferes with eruption of permanent incisors, or bands loosen. Check eruption stage before placement. |
| **Nance holding arch** | Bilateral **maxillary** holding with bands and acrylic palatal button to help resist mesial molar movement. | Acrylic **button compresses or ulcerates palatal mucosa**, traps plaque, or becomes embedded as palatal tissue grows; monitor tissue and cleanability. |
| **Transpalatal arch (TPA)** | Connects maxillary molars with a palatal wire; often used for molar control, rotation, or anchorage, depending on design and stability. | Wire impinges on palatal soft tissue or is distorted/broken; assumption that a TPA always provides the same anchorage as a Nance button. |
| **Removable plate / Hawley-type appliance** | Retention or selected space maintenance when compliance, clasp retention, occlusion, and successor eruption permit. | Poor seating, fractured clasps, poor retention, plaque buildup, occlusal interference, low wear compliance, or lost appliance. Not reliable when the child cannot consistently wear it. |
| **Space regainer (active plate / screw / spring)** | Tooth movement to **recover** space already lost after mesial migration; requires diagnosis and monitored force. | Prescribing passive maintenance after measured space loss; overly rapid activation, anchorage loss, soft-tissue trauma, or lack of reevaluation. |
| **Hyrax / rapid maxillary expander (RME)** | Fixed expansion screw with molar anchorage used for indicated **skeletal transverse deficiency**, selected by growth and orthodontic assessment. | Overactivation without follow-up, unilateral band loosening, screw failure, palatal ulceration, or assuming dental crossbite always equals skeletal constriction. |
| **Fan-type palatal expander** | Fan-hinged expansion pattern for selected patients requiring **greater anterior than posterior** expansion; specialist-controlled, especially in cleft care. | Using a fan expander for **uniform** transverse deficiency without assessing desired expansion pattern; tissue impingement or poor fixation. |
| **Pendulum appliance** | Intraoral fixed appliance for selected **maxillary molar distalization**; molar movement uses anchorage from a palatal acrylic button and other supporting teeth. | Palatal acrylic ulceration, inadequate anchorage/undesired anterior tipping, loss of cement, wire displacement; do not mistake it for a passive space maintainer. |
| **Habit-breaking appliance / tongue crib** | Selected persistent sucking/tongue habits when counseling alone is insufficient, after assessing growth, airway and patient cooperation. | Sharp wire causing tongue trauma, impaired oral function, or assuming an appliance treats a skeletal open bite without addressing etiology. |
| **Reverse-pull facemask** | Selected early **skeletal Class III / maxillary deficiency** cases under orthodontic supervision, usually using intraoral anchorage. | Poor pads/skin breakdown, incorrect elastic attachment or force line, poor wear compliance, or treating all Class III cases identically. |
| **2 × 4 appliance** | Early fixed limited orthodontics: 2 first permanent molar bands/tubes and 4 incisor brackets; selected anterior crossbite/alignment cases. | Starting before appropriate teeth have erupted, ignoring inadequate clearance/trauma risk, or using it when functional/skeletal assessment indicates another approach. |

### Appliance selection — four high-yield clinical decisions

1. **Primary first molar lost early, first permanent molar erupted, unilateral space still present:** consider an appropriate band-and-loop or crown-and-loop depending on abutment restorability.
2. **Primary second molar lost before first permanent molar erupts:** evaluate a **distal-shoe** indication and related subgingival risks; do not assume a passive band-and-loop can guide an unerupted distal first molar.
3. **Bilateral mandibular primary molar loss, first permanent molars already erupted:** assess a **passive lingual holding arch** and whether permanent incisors have erupted adequately for a safe design.
4. **Mesial drift and space loss already occurred:** **space regaining and orthodontic assessment**, not merely a passive space maintainer.

### Pediatric appliance troubleshooting checklist

- **Wrong appliance or timing:** no erupted distal abutment; successor erupting into wire; space already lost; arch/dentition stage inappropriate.
- **Retention failure:** loose/broken band or crown, washed-out cement, distorted spring/loop, lost removable plate.
- **Soft-tissue injury:** loop or arch wire submerged in mucosa, palatal acrylic impingement, ulcer, gingival inflammation, sharp wire end.
- **Iatrogenic tooth movement:** molar tipping, unintended incisor proclination, excessive activation, inadequate anchorage.
- **Hygiene/caries:** food or plaque retention, demineralization around bands, caries under loosened cement, inability to clean under acrylic.
- **Follow-up lapse:** eruption changes not monitored, appliance retained past its indication, no radiographic/clinical assessment when needed.

**Exam reasoning:** State **(1) the intended function, (2) which design/clinical finding is unsafe, (3) the likely harm, and (4) the immediate corrective action.** For a loose, embedded, broken, or ulcerating appliance, arrange prompt assessment and repair/removal as appropriate rather than encouraging continued unattended wear.

## Rapid recall
- **Molar Class II?** Lower first molar is more distal relative to upper first molar.
- **Overjet?** Horizontal overlap.
- **Posterior crossbite with shift?** Early orthodontic evaluation.
- **Premature primary second molar loss before first molar eruption?** Consider distal-shoe indications.
- **Main goal of a space maintainer?** Preserve existing arch length, not create space.

- **Pediatric appliance errors?** Identify a wrong indication, loss of retention, soft-tissue injury, eruption obstruction, unintended movement, or hygiene failure.
- **Band-and-loop vs distal shoe?** Erupted molar/adequate abutment vs unerupted first permanent molar needing guided eruption.
- **Nance vs TPA?** Nance has palatal acrylic button (watch soft-tissue contact); TPA uses cross-palatal wire.
- **Hyrax vs fan?** Broad expansion vs selected anterior-biased expansion pattern.
- **Space has already been lost?** Consider regaining space, not simply holding what remains.
