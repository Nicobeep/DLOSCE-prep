# Endodontics
**Weight: 8% · Official domains:** diagnosis, treatment planning, emergency management, post-treatment evaluation.

## 1. Make TWO diagnoses
- **Pulpal diagnosis:** normal pulp; reversible pulpitis; symptomatic/asymptomatic irreversible pulpitis; pulp necrosis; previously treated; previously initiated therapy (traditional terminology).
- **Apical diagnosis:** normal apical tissues; symptomatic/asymptomatic apical periodontitis; acute/chronic apical abscess; condensing osteitis (traditional terminology).
- The AAE/ESE has been updating endodontic diagnostic terminology; verify terminology in the **current ADEX/JCNDE materials** for the exam cycle rather than assuming a change has been implemented.
- Cold and EPT evaluate **sensibility/neural response**, not direct pulpal blood supply; results require controls and a full clinical assessment.
- **Percussion** assesses periapical periodontal ligament inflammation; **palpation** and **probing** add information.
- Radiographic periapical changes may **lag behind** symptoms. A normal radiograph does not exclude pulpal/apical disease.

## 2. Symptom pattern recognition
- **Brief cold pain that resolves on stimulus removal:** can support reversible pulpitis, depending on the complete history.
- **Spontaneous/lingering thermal pain:** favors symptomatic irreversible pulpitis; confirm with tests and context.
- **No response to cold/EPT:** suggests necrosis in the right context, but false negatives exist in calcified/traumatized teeth.
- **Pain on biting or percussion:** may indicate symptomatic apical periodontitis, cracked tooth or high restoration; differentiate.
- **Sinus tract:** trace with gutta-percha and image when possible to identify source; it may coexist with chronic apical abscess.
- **Deep narrow isolated probing defect:** consider vertical root fracture; not pathognomonic.

## 3. Emergency treatment logic
- **Symptomatic irreversible pulpitis:** definitive care such as pulpotomy/pulpectomy/root canal or extraction as indicated; appropriate analgesia; **not routine antibiotics** for pain alone.
- **Pulp necrosis + localized acute apical abscess:** drainage and definitive dental care; systemic antibiotics only when indicated by relevant guidance and factors.
- **Systemic involvement (fever, malaise, spreading infection):** systemic evaluation, urgent dental treatment and antibiotics when indicated; airway red flags require emergency escalation.
- **Traumatic tooth injury:** determine luxation/avulsion type, root maturity, timing and appropriate current trauma guidelines.

## 4. Treatment quality and follow-up
- Core concepts: **access, canal anatomy, working length, chemo-mechanical cleaning, irrigation safety, intracanal disinfection, obturation, coronal seal**.
- A **rubber dam** is standard isolation for nonsurgical endodontics.
- **Persistent post-treatment apical disease** requires diagnosis: residual infection, missed canal, coronal leakage, fracture or non-endodontic pathology.
- Assess whether **retreatment, apical surgery, monitoring or extraction** best fits restorable tooth and patient goals.
- Radiographic lesion healing takes time; compare serial films and clinical signs.

## Rapid recall
- **Lingering cold pain?** Symptomatic irreversible pulpitis is a key differential.
- **Cold-negative + PA lesion?** Necrosis with apical disease is likely, but corroborate.
- **Pain on percussion?** Apical involvement, not necessarily necrotic pulp.
- **Endodontic antibiotic for uncomplicated pulpitis?** No.
- **Root canal aftercare?** Definitive coronal restoration and follow-up.
