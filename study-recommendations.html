Study the decisions the exam tests—not just facts.

The Dental Licensure Objective Structured Clinical Examination (DLOSCE) is the computer-based clinical judgment component of the ADEX Dental Examination. It evaluates whether a candidate can reason through entry-level dental care decisions using patient histories, clinical findings, imaging, and visual information. It is not a hands-on typodont exercise.

01 / Know the examination

What are you being asked to do?

Think of each item as a concise patient-care decision: identify what matters in the patient box, interpret findings, prioritize diagnosis and management, and choose the safest, most appropriate response.

01

Read the patient box

Age, medical conditions, medications, allergies, chief complaint, history, and current findings may change the correct answer. Never ignore contraindications or urgent red flags.

02

Interpret clinical evidence

Work from radiographs, clinical photographs, periodontal/endodontic charts, and manipulable 3D models. Combine imaging with history and examination; do not diagnose from the image alone.

03

Select the best decision

Expect single-response, multiple-response, and prescription tasks. Ask which choice is indicated, not merely technically possible.

Important multiple-response behavior: Some items allow partial credit, but selecting an incorrect answer can result in no credit for that item. The precise rules vary by item; study the official practice examples and follow each question's instructions. The platform's own question bank currently supports single-best-answer cases, while the Rx simulator is a separate simplified exercise.

Four high-return clinical skills

  • Diagnosis versus management: separately identify the most likely diagnosis and the next best action.
  • Restorability and prognosis: connect caries, pulpal status, periodontal support, remaining tooth structure, and prosthetic planning.
  • Risk triage: identify airway compromise, systemic involvement, drug interactions, and suspicious oral lesions before routine care.
  • Image + patient box integration: support visual interpretation with clinical data rather than guessing from appearance alone.

For shorter days, compress the blocks proportionally; for longer days, add a second subject or a timed set rather than passive rereading.

Flexible four-phase study roadmap

Phase 1Diagnose gaps

Read the official guide, attempt an untimed baseline set in each domain, and start a miss log. Learn the image controls and prescription interface.

Phase 2Build a first pass

Cover all nine high-yield modules, spending more time on restorative/prosthodontics. Practice diagnosis, management, and patient-box interpretation daily.

Phase 3Practice under constraints

Complete mixed timed sets, repeated case reviews, real radiograph drills and independent Rx practice. Verify uncertain answers in clinical sources.

Phase 4Close gaps and rehearse

Repeat your weakest topics with fresh cases. Use official sample question formats, the Prometric 3D tutorial, and a final test-day checklist.

05 / Better study method

Make every practice question informative

Use the four-pass question method

  1. Identify the exact task: diagnose, interpret, choose a treatment, or triage.
  2. Extract decisive patient-box clues: age, symptoms, meds, allergies, images, prior care.
  3. Choose the most appropriate response before viewing explanations.
  4. Explain why the alternatives are wrong and write down the missed principle.

Keep a mistake log

Record: subject, clinical clue missed, correct principle, and why you missed it (knowledge, image reading, patient-box omission, over-selection, pacing). Review these notes 1, 3, and 7 days later.

Practice beyond single-answer items

Use official examples to learn multiple-response instructions and grading. Practice selecting only clearly supported options and deliberately filling out all prescription fields.

Use authentic visuals deliberately

For each real radiograph or clinical photo: first describe findings without naming the diagnosis, then generate a differential, choose any needed clinical tests, and only then decide management. Use licensed clinical images or official linked examples—not fabricated clinical pictures.
