# Prosthodontics
**Weight: 19% · Official domains:** removable, fixed, implants.

## 1. Removable partial dentures — design logic
- **Kennedy I:** bilateral distal extension. **II:** unilateral distal extension. **III:** unilateral bounded edentulous space. **IV:** single anterior edentulous space crossing the midline.
- Under **Applegate rules**, classify after planned extractions; the most posterior edentulous area determines class (with Class IV having no modifications).
- **Rests provide support; direct retainers resist dislodgment; reciprocation counters clasp forces; bracing resists horizontal forces; guide planes help control path of insertion.**
- **Indirect retention** becomes important in distal-extension RPDs to resist rotation away from the mucosa around the fulcrum line.
- Distal-extension support is shared between **teeth and soft tissues**; assess functional movement and load distribution.
- **Surveying** establishes a feasible path of insertion, undercut location, guiding-plane preparation, and clasp design.
- A **major connector** should be rigid and well placed relative to the gingiva, relief areas, and tissue anatomy; design is specific to the arch.
- **RPD case analysis:** evaluate abutment prognosis, caries risk, periodontal support, oral hygiene, opposing occlusion, and patient dexterity.

## 2. Complete dentures
- Retention = resistance to displacement **away** from tissue; support = resistance to **toward** tissue; stability = resistance to **lateral** displacement.
- **Peripheral seal, border extension and posterior palatal seal** are important for maxillary denture retention; overextension can cause sore spots/dislodgment.
- Identify **vertical dimension**, **centric relation**, smile line, phonetics, lip support and neutral zone during jaw relations/try-in.
- Denture sore spots may reflect **overextended borders, pressure areas or occlusal discrepancies**; identify cause before indiscriminate adjustment.
- **Severely resorbed ridges** compromise support and stability; discuss prognosis and alternatives.
- **Immediate dentures** have significant early tissue changes and may need tissue conditioning/reline.

## 3. Fixed crowns and bridges
- Evaluate **restorability**, periodontal prognosis, crown-root ratio, mobility, endodontic status, missing teeth, occlusion and patient preferences before selecting fixed versus removable versus implant therapy.
- **Retention/resistance:** shorter wall height and excessive taper reduce resistance to dislodgment; auxiliary features may be needed when feasible.
- **Pontic design** should support hygiene and tissue health; avoid creating uncleanable contours.
- **Biologic/esthetic margin trade-off:** a subgingival margin is not inherently better; consider tissues, caries, isolation and restorative material.
- **Ferrule** and sound tooth structure are central to the success of many post-endodontic crowns. A post generally **retains a core**, not strengthens the root.
- **Occlusion:** premature contacts, nonaxial forces and parafunction can affect prosthesis longevity.

## 4. Implant restorations and complications
- Differentiate **peri-implant health**, **peri-implant mucositis** (inflammation without progressive supporting bone loss), and **peri-implantitis** (inflammation with progressive supporting bone loss).
- Assess peri-implant probing, BOP/suppuration, radiographic bone level, tissue contour, oral hygiene and prosthesis accessibility.
- **Screw loosening, screw fracture, porcelain chipping, prosthesis fracture** are mechanical/prosthetic complications; **infection/bone loss** are biological problems.
- Implant success depends on prosthetically driven planning and long-term maintenance, not only implant integration.
- **Lack of keratinized mucosa** may affect comfort and hygiene for some patients; assess individually.

## 5. Key comparisons and exam traps
- **Kennedy II vs III:** distal extension vs bounded space.
- **RPD indirect retention:** helps when rotation about fulcrum line is possible; not a substitute for adequate support.
- **Poor complete denture retention:** assess borders, seal, base adaptation, saliva and occlusion before adding adhesive.
- **Implant BOP alone:** suggests mucosal inflammation, does not alone confirm peri-implantitis.
- **Missing tooth replacement:** diagnose the patient’s needs, not a predetermined treatment modality.

## Rapid recall
- **Bilateral distal extension?** Kennedy I.
- **Unilateral distal extension?** Kennedy II.
- **Anterior space crosses midline?** Kennedy IV.
- **What does the rest provide?** Support.
- **Why survey a cast?** Path of insertion and usable retentive undercuts.
