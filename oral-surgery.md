# Oral surgery
**Weight: 9% · Official domains:** diagnosis, treatment planning, extractions.

## 1. Surgical assessment and referral
- Before extraction: **medical history, medications, bleeding risk, allergies, airway, cardiopulmonary status, imaging, tooth anatomy, infection, consent and alternatives**.
- Assess **root curvature, hypercementosis, proximity to inferior alveolar canal, maxillary sinus, adjacent tooth, unerupted position and available access**.
- Consider whether referral is indicated because of **full bony impaction, nerve proximity, complex medical status, limited access, expected complications or operator skill/scope**.
- **Asymptomatic fully impacted third molar** does not automatically require extraction. Evaluate pathologic findings, risks, patient age, access, patient preference and surveillance options.
- **Coronectomy** may be considered in selected vital mandibular third molars at high risk to the inferior alveolar nerve; contraindications and follow-up are case-dependent.

## 2. Odontogenic infections — urgent triage
- **Airway first:** stridor, drooling, dysphagia, voice change, floor-of-mouth elevation, rapidly progressive swelling or systemic toxicity require immediate emergency escalation.
- **Source control** (endodontic treatment or extraction, drainage as indicated) is central for an odontogenic infection.
- Antibiotics are **adjunctive**, not a substitute for source control, and are not routinely indicated for localized pulpal pain.
- Evaluate **trismus**, fascial space, fever, immunocompromise, swelling location and progression.
- **Ludwig angina** involves bilateral submandibular/sublingual/submental spaces and may threaten the airway.
- Admit or escalate complex cases based on airway, hydration, sepsis, spread, comorbidities and resources.

## 3. Bleeding and systemic risk
- Review antithrombotic medication type, indication, renal/hepatic function, procedural bleeding risk and prescriber advice.
- **Do not automatically discontinue warfarin, DOACs or antiplatelet drugs** for all dental procedures. Individualize and prioritize local hemostasis.
- Local hemostatic measures include **pressure, sutures, absorbable hemostatic materials**, and agents appropriate to the situation.
- Evaluate risk of **MRONJ** in patients with antiresorptive/antiangiogenic exposure; route, indication, duration, comorbidities and disease context matter.
- Check for **bisphosphonate/denosumab** history before invasive care; coordination may be required, especially in oncology patients.

## 4. Complications and postoperative management
- **Alveolar osteitis:** severe pain several days after extraction with loss of normal clot protection; treat with gentle local care and analgesia, not routine antibiotics in the absence of infection.
- **Oroantral communication:** suspect after maxillary posterior extractions; assess size and symptoms, provide precautions and refer/repair when indicated.
- **Postextraction infection:** differentiate normal postoperative inflammatory signs from spreading infection, fever or purulence.
- **Paresthesia/anesthesia:** document baseline/postoperative nerve findings and follow an appropriate referral/review plan.
- Prevent medication overuse; use current analgesic guidance and individual contraindications.

## Rapid recall
- **Floor-of-mouth swelling + dysphagia?** Emergency airway evaluation.
- **Dry socket?** Local supportive treatment; not automatic antibiotics.
- **All DOACs stopped for extractions?** No; individualize.
- **Third molar near IAN canal?** Assess risk; additional imaging only if it changes management.
- **Deep-space infection?** Urgent care, source control, airway assessment.
