Prescription tasks — antibiotics & analgesics

Weight: 5% · Official domains: antibiotic, analgesic prescribing. The published guide describes a prescription selection task; the official practice guide is essential for the exact interface and scoring.

1. Prescription checklist — every task

  • Read the patient box before choosing a drug: age, pregnancy, allergies and reaction, current medications, renal/hepatic disease, anticoagulants, systemic infection, weight and comorbidities.
  • Decide whether medication is indicated at all; the right answer may be no antibiotic in a pain-only case.
  • Specify generic drug, strength, dose per administration, route, frequency, duration or stop rule, quantity, refills and instructions.
  • Math: quantity = units per dose × doses per day × days when fixed-duration dosing is used. PRN regimens require clear maximum intended usage.
  • Check duplicate active ingredients (e.g., acetaminophen in combination products), renal safety with NSAIDs, liver disease, interactions and allergy.
  • Distinguish a correct prescription from an acceptable alternative and from a contraindicated option based on case details.

2. Antibiotic stewardship

  • Symptomatic irreversible pulpitis and most localized pulpal/periapical pain in immunocompetent adults → definitive dental care and analgesia, not routine systemic antibiotics.
  • Pulp necrosis with acute apical abscess and systemic involvement can warrant antibiotics alongside urgent definitive dental care.
  • A guideline-based example in a non-allergic, immunocompetent adult with indicated systemic infection: amoxicillin 500 mg orally three times daily for 3–7 days, reevaluate within 3 days and stop 24 hours after symptoms resolve; confirm current ADA guide and exact scenario.
  • A patient with true immediate-type beta-lactam allergy requires individual alternative selection; some alternatives carry important adverse-effect or resistance concerns.
  • Dental prophylaxis for certain cardiac conditions and invasive procedures is a different indication from treatment of established infection. Do not confuse them.
  • A tooth with a localized abscess but no systemic signs generally needs local source control first; systemic antibiotics depend on access to definitive care and patient-specific guideline recommendations.

3. Analgesic prescribing

  • For many adults/adolescents with acute dental pain, NSAIDs alone or combined with acetaminophen are first-line when appropriate under the ADA’s 2024 guideline.
  • NSAID cautions: renal disease, active GI ulcer/bleeding, certain anticoagulants, significant cardiovascular risk, pregnancy timing, allergy and drug interactions.
  • Acetaminophen cautions: liver disease, heavy alcohol exposure, duplicate OTC combination drugs; respect patient-specific and product maximum daily doses.
  • Avoid reflexive opioid prescribing; if exceptional use is considered, use current guidance, risk evaluation, minimal quantity and required rules.
  • Treat the underlying cause. Analgesics relieve pain but are not substitutes for endodontic therapy, restoration, drainage or extraction as appropriate.

4. Calculation practice

  • 500 mg tablet, 1 tablet TID for 7 days → 1 × 3 × 7 = 21 tablets.
  • 400 mg tablet, 1 tablet q6h PRN for up to 3 days → maximum 1 × 4 × 3 = 12 tablets.
  • 250 mg tablets, ordered 500 mg per dose BID for 5 days → 2 × 2 × 5 = 20 tablets.
  • Always distinguish a dose from a tablet strength: two 250 mg tablets equals one 500 mg dose.
  • When a problem specifies stop upon symptom resolution, the maximum quantity may still be based on the prescribed upper range; comply with the stated task instructions.

5. High-yield traps

  • Antibiotic for irreversible pulpitis alone? No.
  • One sulfa allergy = penicillin allergy? No; read the actual allergy history.
  • NSAID in active GI bleed? Avoid; choose appropriate alternative and assess.
  • Quantity for PRN medication: calculate based on maximum use and duration if specified.
  • Never assume a single regimen fits every patient: the patient box controls selection.

Rapid recall

  • Dose quantity formula? Units/dose × doses/day × days.
  • First-line acute dental pain? Usually nonopioid (NSAID ± acetaminophen) when appropriate.
  • Systemic antibiotic for pain alone? Usually no.
  • Systemic signs with acute odontogenic abscess? Urgent definitive care plus indicated systemic management.
  • Prescription fields? Drug, strength, per-administration units, frequency, duration/stop rule, quantity, route and relevant instructions.
