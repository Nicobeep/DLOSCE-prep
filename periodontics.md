# Periodontics
**Weight: 10% · Official domains:** diagnosis, treatment planning, etiology.

## 1. Core measurements and interpretation
- **Probing depth (PD)** is the gingival margin to pocket base; **clinical attachment loss (CAL)** is CEJ to pocket base.
- If gingival margin is **apical to CEJ**: CAL = PD + recession. If margin is **coronal to CEJ**: CAL = PD − gingival enlargement.
- **Bleeding on probing** indicates inflammation/risk, not by itself the definition of periodontitis.
- Interpret **radiographic bone loss** with clinical findings. Conventional two-dimensional films may under- or overestimate complex defects.
- **Furcation involvement**, mobility, drifting, tooth loss attributable to periodontitis and complexity affect staging and treatment.
- Common risk modifiers: **tobacco exposure, glycemic control, plaque-retentive factors, previous progression, supportive care adherence**.

## 2. 2017 World Workshop staging and grading framework
- **Stage I:** initial periodontitis; **II:** moderate; **III:** severe with potential for additional tooth loss; **IV:** severe with complex rehabilitation needs.
- Staging uses worst interdental CAL, radiographic bone loss and tooth loss **due to periodontitis**; complexity factors can shift stage.
- **Grade A:** slow progression; **B:** moderate; **C:** rapid. Evidence includes direct longitudinal change or indirect % bone loss/age and risk modifiers.
- Grade assignment should be individualized; **diabetes control and smoking** can modify grade under the classification framework.
- **Extent:** localized (<30% of teeth involved), generalized (≥30%), or molar/incisor pattern, when applicable.
- **Periodontal health on an intact periodontium** differs from health on a reduced periodontium after successful periodontal therapy.

## 3. Non-surgical treatment sequence
1. Establish diagnosis, periodontal chart, relevant radiographs, and patient risk factors.
2. Discuss prognosis, self-care, interdental cleaning, smoking cessation and diabetes control.
3. Remove/reduce subgingival biofilm and calculus with appropriate instrumentation and address plaque-retentive factors.
4. **Reevaluate** tissue response: BOP, PD, residual pockets, compliance and overall disease control.
5. Consider surgery, targeted management or specialist referral for residual complex defects.
6. Establish **supportive periodontal therapy** intervals based on individual risk and stability.

## 4. Prognosis and implant overlap
- Tooth prognosis is not based on PD alone: weigh attachment/bone support, furcation, root anatomy, mobility, restorability, endodontic prognosis, patient factors and maintenance.
- **Vertical/intrabony defects** may be considered for regenerative procedures in selected cases; site morphology matters.
- **Mucogingival recession** requires evaluation of tissue thickness, keratinized tissue, inflammation, esthetic and sensitivity concerns.
- Peri-implant mucositis is primarily an inflammatory lesion without progressive supporting bone loss; peri-implantitis has progressive bone loss with inflammatory findings.

## Exam traps
- Deep pocket without CAL may reflect gingival enlargement rather than periodontitis.
- Recession with shallow PD can still represent significant attachment loss.
- One bleeding site is not sufficient to assign stage or grade.
- A radiograph cannot determine probing depth or distinguish all endo-perio lesions.

## Rapid recall
- **PD 4 mm + recession 3 mm?** CAL 7 mm.
- **Staging describes?** Severity/complexity.
- **Grading describes?** Progression and modifiers.
- **Localized threshold?** Less than 30% of teeth involved.
- **After scaling and root planing?** Reevaluate and plan maintenance.
