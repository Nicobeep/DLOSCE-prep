# Oral pathology, orofacial pain & TMD
**Weight: 13% · Official domains:** oral medicine/pathology; orofacial pain and TMD.

## 1. Oral lesion recognition and red flags
- Describe **site, size, shape, color, distribution, borders, surface, palpation, duration, symptoms and progression** before assigning a diagnosis.
- **Persistent unexplained ulcer, induration, erythroplakia, unexplained leukoplakia, fixation, neck node or cranial-nerve change:** prompt specialist evaluation and often biopsy.
- **Leukoplakia is a clinical descriptive diagnosis** for a predominantly white lesion not characterized as another disease. It does **not** mean cancer, but dysplasia/cancer must be excluded when indicated.
- **Erythroplakia** is concerning for dysplasia/malignancy; do not observe casually.
- **Scrapable white plaques** may suggest candidiasis; **non-scrapable** lesions require a broader differential (frictional keratosis, lichen planus, leukoplakia, etc.).
- **Bilateral reticular white striae** support oral lichen planus, but erosive/unilateral/atypical lesions require careful assessment and sometimes biopsy.
- **Recurrent aphthous ulceration** typically involves nonkeratinized mucosa; distinguish from herpes patterns and persistent unilateral lesions.
- **Traumatic ulcer** should improve with removal of a confirmed irritant; lack of healing requires reassessment and possible biopsy.

## 2. Common lesions and differentials
- **Mucocele:** often lower lip, fluctuant/translucent, from minor salivary gland duct injury.
- **Ranula:** floor-of-mouth mucocele; size and extension affect management.
- **Periapical/radicular cyst:** associated with nonvital tooth; **dentigerous cyst:** surrounds the crown of an unerupted tooth, attaching near CEJ; imaging is not histologic diagnosis.
- **Odontogenic keratocyst:** may grow anteroposteriorly with less expansion than expected; requires specialist management and surveillance.
- **Ameloblastoma:** often multilocular posterior mandible; local aggressiveness and recurrence risk require definitive planning.
- **Sialolithiasis:** episodic gland pain/swelling, often with meals; assess gland, duct and infection signs.
- **Oral candidiasis:** consider antibiotics, steroids, diabetes, dentures, immunosuppression and xerostomia.

## 3. Orofacial pain
- Distinguish **odontogenic**, **myofascial/TMD**, **neuropathic**, **headache-related**, **sinus** and **referred** pain using history and examination.
- **Trigeminal neuralgia:** brief electric-shock-like episodes triggered by light touch or simple activities; requires medical/dental differential and referral as needed.
- **Persistent pain after normal dental evaluation:** avoid irreversible treatment based solely on location of pain.
- **Pain with palpation of masticatory muscles reproducing familiar pain** supports a muscular TMD component.
- **Localized tooth pain on biting/release:** consider crack, occlusion or periapical inflammation; perform pulp tests and imaging.

## 4. TMD decision framework
- Screen for **pain with jaw function, locking, limitation, joint noises, trauma, parafunction and headaches**.
- Clicking without pain or dysfunction does not automatically mandate treatment.
- **Disc displacement with reduction** often has clicking; **without reduction** may cause limited opening/closed lock.
- First-line management for many uncomplicated TMD presentations is **reversible and conservative**: education, self-management, habit modification, exercises/physical therapy, short-term appropriate medications.
- Avoid immediate irreversible occlusal adjustment or extensive dental reconstruction to treat an uncomplicated TMD pain complaint.
- Refer for red flags such as progressive neurological deficits, unexplained swelling, marked asymmetry, systemic symptoms or suspected non-TMD pathology.

## Rapid recall
- **Non-scrapable persistent tongue lesion?** Differential + prompt evaluation; biopsy if suspicious.
- **Erythroplakia?** High concern, specialist evaluation.
- **Pericoronal radiolucency around impacted crown?** Dentigerous cyst among differentials.
- **Familiar pain reproduced by masseter palpation?** Myalgia-associated TMD possible.
- **Uncomplicated TMD first-line?** Conservative, reversible care.
